* Reviewer #1

In this manuscript the authors present evidence from three experiments testing the predictions of a model extension describing decision-making in delayed-report paradigms, including when the response-mapping is unknown until the delayed response cue. This is important for understanding how models of decision-making (mostly formulated in immediate report conditions) could also describe decisions when the behavioural response is delayed (or even when no behavioural response is required).

On first read of this manuscript I was in the mood to read some high-quality, well-written, rigorous science. This would normally bias my review to be more critical. Instead, this manuscript surpassed my expectations. The manuscript is impressive in explaining complicated concepts with clarity, and presenting detailed analyses in a well-ordered, and comprehensible manner. The experimental methods are thorough in testing different aspects of the model predictions and allowing room for model falsification. The discussion is comprehensive in describing not only the findings and implications, but also potential limitations of the work. I only have minor comments. 

Figure 1 very clearly presents the predictions for the mean premotor time and mean motor time. I was wondering if the predictions concerning the rate of partial muscle activation could also be incorporated, for example, with a vertical arrow from the accumulated evidence to the gate.  Then this figure could also be referenced in explaining the predictions for the rate of partial muscle activation under Experiment 1.

* Reviewer #2: 

The paper presents an interesting modeling extension of the gated cascade diffusion model (GCD) to delayed response paradigms during decision-making tasks. The work is well-framed in the current literature and well-argued for the necessity of the new model. The experimental results are interesting in relation to the model. I feel as though the work is an important contribution to the study of decision-making.

The main problem I see with the paper is that I'm not sure how the GCD model is falsifiable. The authors are presenting the experimental results in support of the GCD, but do not offer (clear) examples of how possible experimental data could have shown evidence against the GCD.

** Major

The theory does not (cannot) make separate behavior predictions for a continuous accumulation of evidence after the stimulus onset to a fixed threshold. For instance, in Figure 1B if evidence accumulation continued in the light green line to the fixed threshold immediately before the RS, the behavioural predictions would be the same.

=R2.2 TBD=

Top of page 11: If the choice is already made and the stimulus-response mapping is known, what could the second evidence accumulation step after the RS plausibly be for?

=R2.3 TBD=

It is not clear to me from Figure 1 nor the text how the GCD model makes predictions about the slope of muscle activation nor the number of partial muscle activations.

=R2.4 TBD=

It is also not clear from Figure 1 how the theory is actually simulated with noise. What do trials of the GCD actually look like? Diffusion evidence accumulation paths are extremely noisy with a constant drift rate across trials (see Figure 2a by Nunez et al., 2024 preprint) and not a good reflection of the drift rate itself. Does the GCD predict linear ballistic accumulation or diffusion accumulation or some other form of accumulation?

=R2.5 TBD=

Minor

Bottom of page 3: The authors should define the two signals here. I am only familiar with the CPP, as I am sure other readers will be. Therefore, I'm not sure what the second motor response signal is. If it is the (Lateralized) Readiness Potential, then other authors could be cited as well, such as Gluth et al. (2013) and Lui et al. (2021). It is also noted that the (L)RP rises to a negative peak, though negative vs positive is not so important in scalp electrophysiology (and if anything negative potentials are thought to reflect increased activity in classical ERP work). The Limitations and future directions section later suggests that the authors may be instead referring to a mu/beta power increase, though the same papers (Rogge et al., 2022; Twomey et al., 2016) were not cited above in the introduction.

=R2.6=

From the equations on page 6, I expected the dark green line in the lower quadrant of Figure 1A to start accumulating when the upper dark green line crosses g. However, this is not the case as the bottom green line accumulates clearly much earlier. Is this a mistake in the figure?

=R2.7=

Code was not made available (to reviewers): https://osf.io/vsm53/.

=R2.8=

In Experiment 1, the fact that the RS onset delays were fixed in a block means that participants could predict the delay ahead of time. Does this affect the model results? How do the authors know that the same predictions would hold to an experiment without predictable delays? Participants could strategically stop accumulating evidence (in working memory after the stimulus is off) if they can predict the RS onset.

=R2.9=

The paragraph that begins in the middle of page 17 meanders and it is not clear if the authors are making a prediction about the data given the GCD in RS tasks or just discussing the literature.

=R2.10=

Figure 2 C; Figure 3 A,B,C; Figure 4 B: p-value significance and/or Bayes Factors should be shown on this plot in some way. The standard error bars are not useful for this.

=R2.11=

Experiment 2 Predictions on page 31: The authors make the assumption that participants cannot sample from working memory, e.g. a shorter stimulus duration should be more difficult. I'm not sure this is justified.
